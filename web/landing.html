How it works

See. Judge. Respond. Record.

One loop runs several times a second, from the camera to the lamp and back.

  1. 01

    See

    A USB camera feeds YOLO11, which finds people, vehicles, knives, scissors and bats in each frame.

  2. 02

    Judge

    The risk engine weighs what it sees with the time of day, PIR motion and the light sensor, and settles on LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH.

  3. 03

    Respond

    An ESP32 dims or floods the street light and sounds the buzzer, within a heartbeat of the decision.

  4. 04

    Record

    Every escalation is saved with a snapshot, a location and the reasons, ready to review, report or send.

Risk levels

Three levels. Three responses.

Clear rules, every one adjustable in Settings. A weapon has to be seen twice before the alarm sounds.

LOW

Street clear

Nobody around. The lamp dims to 20% at night and switches off by day.

  • Light 20% · night
  • Buzzer off
MEDIUM

Someone's here

A person or motion at night, a crowd, or someone lingering. Full light, event recorded.

  • Light 100%
  • Incident + snapshot
HIGH

Weapon seen

A weapon confirmed across frames. Full light, buzzer on, evidence saved, contacts alerted.

  • Light 100% · buzzer on
  • Alert to contacts
Hardware

A real lamp, not a mock-up.

An ESP32 drives the light with PWM dimming and the buzzer, and reports PIR motion and LDR light levels twice a second over USB. If the dashboard goes quiet for five seconds, the lamp keeps working on its own sensors.

Street light LEDGPIO 25PWM
Piezo buzzerGPIO 26out
PIR motionGPIO 27in
LDR light levelGPIO 34ADC
Responsible by design

Fast to react. Careful to accuse.

A camera on a street is a serious thing. These are on by default.

A person confirms the alert

AI can mistake a phone for a knife. HIGH incidents wait for an operator before anyone is messaged, unless you choose automatic.

Video stays on the device

Detection runs locally and the dashboard listens only on this computer. Only an alert you send leaves it.

Evidence expires

Snapshots and records are deleted after 30 days by default, so footage isn't kept longer than it's useful.

Every decision is explained

Each incident keeps the reasons it was raised, so a review starts from facts, not a score.
